Biology. The science of life.

From a single cell to a whole ecosystem, Biology explains how living things are built, how they work and how they depend on each other.

  • O Level
  • A Level
  • ZIMSEC · Cambridge-aligned
Download the study guide See the topics

Big ideas

Three ideas that hold it all together.

Master these and every topic in Biology becomes easier to understand and remember.

  1. 01

    The cell is the unit of life

    Every living thing is made of cells. Plant and animal cells both have a nucleus, cytoplasm and a cell membrane; plant cells also have a cell wall, chloroplasts and a large vacuole.

  2. 02

    Energy flows, matter cycles

    Plants capture light energy in photosynthesis. Energy passes along food chains and is lost as heat, while carbon, nitrogen and water are recycled again and again.

  3. 03

    Variation and inheritance

    Genes on chromosomes pass from parents to offspring. Variation, together with natural selection, explains how populations change over time.

Key terms

Words to know by heart.

Examiners reward precise language. Learn each definition word for word.

Diffusion
The net movement of particles from a region of higher concentration to a region of lower concentration.
Osmosis
The movement of water molecules from a dilute solution to a more concentrated one, through a partially permeable membrane.
Enzyme
A protein that speeds up a chemical reaction in a living organism without being used up.
Photosynthesis
Green plants make glucose from carbon dioxide and water using light energy, releasing oxygen.
Respiration
The release of energy from glucose, which happens in every living cell.
Homeostasis
Keeping conditions inside the body, such as temperature and water content, within set limits.
Gene
A length of DNA that codes for a protein, and so controls a characteristic.
Ecosystem
A community of living organisms together with their physical environment.

In the laboratory

Learning by doing.

  • Using a light microscope and drawing what you see
  • Food tests: iodine for starch, Benedict’s for reducing sugar, biuret for protein
  • Testing a leaf for starch
  • Investigating osmosis with potato strips
  • Measuring how temperature affects an enzyme
  • Fieldwork with quadrats and keys

Study smarter

Four habits of top students.

  1. Draw and label diagrams from memory, then check them.
  2. Learn definitions word for word: examiners look for key words.
  3. Always link a structure to its function.
  4. Practise reading graphs and tables from experiments.

Check yourself

Try these, then reveal the answers.

Q1Name three structures found in plant cells but not in animal cells.+
Answer

A cell wall, chloroplasts and a large permanent vacuole.

Q2Why does a potato strip placed in a concentrated salt solution get shorter?+
Answer

Water leaves the potato cells by osmosis into the more concentrated solution, so the cells lose water and shrink.

Q3Write the word equation for photosynthesis.+
Answer

carbon dioxide + water → glucose + oxygen (in the presence of light and chlorophyll).
