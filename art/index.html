01Sketch

Graphite pencils

✏️ Graphite pencils: from 9H to 9B

H = hard and light · B = black and soft · HB sits in the middle.
Use 2H–HB for guide lines, HB for everyday sketching and 2B–6B for shading and deep shadows.

💡 Pro tip: start light and build up layers. Pressing hard dents the paper, and dents can't be erased.

02Highlight

Kneaded eraser

🩶 The kneaded eraser: soft, stretchy putty that lifts graphite and charcoal instead of rubbing it away.

1. Knead it soft  2. Pinch a point  3. Dab to lift
Perfect for highlights in eyes, hair and shiny objects.

💡 Pro tip: dab the area 3–4 times, then knead the point back in so you don't spread graphite.

03Clean up

Vinyl eraser

🧽 The vinyl eraser: firm, white and made for clean, complete erasing and crisp edges.

Cut a clean corner for details, and use an eraser pen for fine lines.

💡 Pro tip: use the kneaded eraser while you sketch and save the vinyl eraser for the final clean-up. Brush crumbs away, never wipe with your hand.

04Blend

Blending stump & tortillon

🌫️ Blending stump and tortillon: tightly rolled paper for smooth, soft shading.

Stump: solid, pointed both ends, for big areas.
Tortillon: hollow spiral, one point, for small details.

💡 Pro tip: don't blend with your fingers, because skin oils leave marks. Clean a dirty tip on fine sandpaper.

05Bold darks

Charcoal

🖤 Charcoal: three kinds, from soft smoky grey to the deepest black.

Vine: light, soft, easy to erase.
Compressed: the deepest blacks.
Charcoal pencil: control and detail.

💡 Pro tip: start loose with vine charcoal, then add darks with compressed. Rest your hand on a scrap sheet so you don't smudge.

06Surface

Sketchbook & paper

📓 Sketchbook and paper: weight (gsm) and texture (tooth) change how every tool behaves.

80–120 gsm: pencil sketching
120–160 gsm: ink and markers
200 gsm and up: watercolour and wet media
Smooth paper for ink, toothy paper for charcoal.

💡 Pro tip: date every page. Look back after a month and you'll see how much you've improved.

07Prep

Sharpen your point

📐 Sharpen your point: the shape of the point changes the mark you make.

Sharp point: detail.
Long exposed lead (craft knife): side shading.
Chisel edge (sandpaper block): wide, even strokes.

⚠️ Craft knives only with a teacher. Sharpen over a bin, never over your drawing.

08Ink

Fineliner pens

🖊️ Fineliner pens: water-resistant pigment ink in nib sizes from 0.05 mm to 0.8 mm.

Thin nibs for detail, thick nibs for outlines.
Ink shading: hatching, cross-hatching and stippling.

💡 Pro tip: pencil first, ink over it, let the ink dry fully, then erase the pencil.

09Colour

Coloured pencils

🌈 Coloured pencils: wax-based or oil-based, both layer and mix beautifully.

Wax-based: soft and blends easily.
Oil-based: harder and keeps a sharp point.

💡 Pro tip: layer light to dark with gentle pressure. Burnish (press hard) only at the very end for a smooth, shiny finish.

10Measure

Ruler, set square & compass

📏 Ruler, set square and compass: straight lines, true angles and perfect circles.

One-point perspective: every line going into the distance meets at one vanishing point on the horizon.

Use them for buildings, patterns, geometric art and technical drawing.
