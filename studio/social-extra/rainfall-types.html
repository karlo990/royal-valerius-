Three ways
the sky makes rain.

All rain follows one rule. What pushes the air up gives each type its name.
THE ONE RULE BEHIND ALL RAIN
Air risespushed up by heat, hills or a front
It coolsabout 1 °C for every 100 m
Cloud formsvapour condenses at dew point
Rain fallsdroplets grow heavy enough
1

Convectional rainThe sun pushes it up.

  • The sun heats the ground. The air above warms, expands and rises in thermals.
  • It cools to dew point and condenses into cumulus clouds.
  • Strong uplift builds towering cumulonimbus.
CLOUD

Cumulonimbus

RAIN

Heavy, short afternoon storms · thunder and lightning

WHERE

Zimbabwe, November to March · most of our rain

2

Relief rainA mountain pushes it up.

  • Moist wind is forced up the windward slope of high ground.
  • It cools, condenses and rains on the windward side.
  • Beyond the top the air sinks, warms and dries: the rain shadow.
CLOUD

Cloud caps on the hills

RAIN

Wet windward slopes · dry leeward side

WHERE

Eastern Highlands: Nyanga, Chimanimani · the Save valley lies in the rain shadow

3

Frontal rainCold air pushes it up.

  • Warm, moist air meets a mass of cold, dense air at a front.
  • The lighter warm air is forced to rise over the cold air.
  • It cools slowly into wide grey sheets of nimbostratus.
CLOUD

Nimbostratus

RAIN

Steady, light to moderate · lasts for hours

WHERE

Most of Britain's rain · in Zimbabwe, winter cold fronts bring cool, grey days
