## 1. United States: NASA GLOBE, CoCoRaHS, NOAA/NWS, and named schools

### Takeaway
GLOBE (NASA/NSF-sponsored, worldwide since 1995) and CoCoRaHS (a volunteer rain-gauge network whose data the National Weather Service uses) are the two best-documented US models. Both have students take simple, standard readings at a fixed time every day and upload them to a shared online database that scientists actually use.

### Cited Findings
**GLOBE Program**
- GLOBE was announced by the US Government on Earth Day 1994 and began worldwide implementation in 1995. It is jointly sponsored by NASA and the National Science Foundation, with support from NOAA and the State Department. — [Wikipedia: GLOBE Program](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/GLOBE_Program); [NASA: GLOBE marks 20 years](https://www.nasa.gov/press-release/globe-program-marks-20-years-of-global-earth-science-education)
- Scale: about 112 to 114 countries have participated (figures vary by source and date), with more than 66,000 GLOBE-trained teachers at over 24,000 schools. Students have reported millions of measurements (one source says over 100 million) across Atmosphere/Climate, Hydrology, Soil, Land Cover/Biology and Phenology. — [USF C-IMAGE GLOBE training](https://www.marine.usf.edu/c-image/globe-training/); [About GLOBE](https://globe.gov/web/mission-earth/overview-old/about-us-old/about-globe)
- Countries join through government-to-government agreements, and each Country Partner runs its own in-country activities. A 2017 Botswana school launch event was used to lobby the Botswana government to sign the GLOBE agreement. — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/GLOBE_Program); [Botswana Daily News](https://dailynews.gov.bw/news-detail/38752)
- Atmosphere protocols listed by GLOBE: aerosols, air temperature, barometric pressure, clouds, precipitation, relative humidity, surface temperature, water vapour and wind (plus surface ozone). — [GLOBE Observer news: all protocols open](https://observer.globe.gov/news-events-and-people/news/-/obsnewsdetail/19589576/all-globe-protocols-now-open-to-citizen-scientists); [GLOBE Surface Ozone](https://www.globe.gov/web/atmosphere/protocols/surface-ozone)
- Daily timing: the GLOBE Atmosphere Investigation says precipitation and maximum/minimum air temperature are read daily within one hour of local solar noon. For current air temperature, the thermometer is held in open air, out of direct sun, for at least three minutes. — [MyNASAData GLOBE Connections (volcanic eruptions)](https://mynasadata.larc.nasa.gov/globe-connections/globe-connections-volcanic-eruptions); [GLOBE Connections: air quality](https://mynasadata.larc.nasa.gov/globe-connections/globe-connections-air-quality) [VERIFY the solar-noon wording against the current Max/Min/Current Temperature protocol PDF on the [GLOBE Clouds page](https://www.globe.gov/web/atmosphere/protocols/clouds)]
- Cloud observations: students report which cloud types they can see, the percentage of sky covered, cloud opacity, and sky and surface conditions. If they observe while a satellite passes overhead, NASA emails them a comparison with the satellite data, because "satellites only see the top of the clouds while you see the bottom." GLOBE recommends practising first with the "Estimating Cloud Cover: A Simulation" activity. — [MyNASAData GLOBE Connections](https://mynasadata.larc.nasa.gov/globe-connections/globe-connections-air-quality); [GLOBE Clouds protocol](https://www.globe.gov/web/atmosphere/protocols/clouds)
- Data upload routes: a printable Atmosphere Investigation data sheet, GLOBE Online Data Entry (needs a GLOBE ID), the GLOBE Data Entry app (needs a GLOBE ID), or the free GLOBE Observer app (no ID needed). A December 2025 update added a "GLOBE Data Entry" button in the app for all members, and a newer "Atmosphere Data Entry" section accepts temperature, humidity and pressure alongside cloud photos. — [GLOBE Clouds: Report](https://www.globe.gov/web/s-cool/home/observation-and-reporting/report); [GLOBE blog: new way to submit met observations](https://www.globe.gov/web/globe-international-stem-network/overview/scientists-blogs/-/blogs/82515138/maximized); [GLOBE Observer on the App Store](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/globe-observer/id1090456751)
- Example of a student project outside the US: a Sri Lankan middle school ran GLOBE Air Temperature, Barometric Pressure, Clouds and Relative Humidity protocols at solar noon for about a month. — [GLOBE student research report](https://www.globe.gov/do-globe/research-resources/student-research-reports/projectdetail/globe/mission-earth-report)

**CoCoRaHS (Community Collaborative Rain, Hail and Snow Network)**
- Schools register on the CoCoRaHS website, put a rain gauge outside, and report precipitation daily or nearly daily. A suggested routine: several students read the gauge each morning at the same time and upload it to CoCoRaHS.org, or photograph the reading for the class. — [NSTA: Gauging Rainfall with CoCoRaHS (Feb 2020)](https://www.nsta.org/science-teacher/science-teacher-february-2020/gauging-rainfall-cocorahs)
- Twice a year, in autumn and spring, a "Rain Gauge Week" has registered schools record precipitation for one week. — [Horizon Research CoCoRaHS curriculum](https://horizon-research.com/sbpscience/cocorahs/)
- Horizon Research's month-by-month school curriculum has students compare their data with nearby stations, look at historical records, study rain shadows and the effect of nearby water, and finish with student-led projects. — [Horizon Research](https://horizon-research.com/tl4cs/?p=209)
- Colorado State University gave free rain gauges and training to Colorado K-12 schools from 2011 to 2013. — [CSU press release 2012](https://newsmediarelations.colostate.edu/2012/08/01/colorado-state-university-to-colorado-k-12-teachers-join-volunteer-network-get-free-rain-gauge-and-training/); [CSU 2013](https://newsmediarelations.colostate.edu/2013/09/09/colorado-state-university-empowers-k12-schools-to-help-with-precipitation-monitoring-program/) [VERIFY whether free gauges are still offered]
- National Weather Service use: NWS offices say CoCoRaHS reports help estimate soil saturation and flood thresholds, and heavy-rain reports have alerted forecasters and led them to issue flood or flash-flood warnings. One cited case is a 2008 flash-flood warning in Massachusetts. The data is also used over longer periods to find the areas worst hit by drought, and to check radar rainfall estimates. — [NWS Pendleton CoCoRaHS](https://weather.gov/pdt/cocorahs); [NWS Miami](https://weather.gov/mfl/CoCoRaHS); [NWS Jackson](https://www.weather.gov/jan/cocorahs/); [NWS Greenville-Spartanburg](https://www.weather.gov/gsp/cocorahs)
- One magazine article says about 26,500 observing stations feed NWS flood warnings, with this data used in up to half of those warnings. — [YES! Magazine](https://www.yesmagazine.org/?p=122402) [secondary source; VERIFY]

**NOAA/NWS education and data sharing**
- NOAA runs an education portal, including real-time data collections for classrooms. — [NOAA Education: real-time data](https://www.noaa.gov/education/resource-collections/data/real-time); [NWS Education](https://www.weather.gov/about/education)
- The NWS National School Outreach programme offers free virtual classroom talks, including an "Ask-a-Meteorologist" session, for elementary to high school students. — [NWS El Paso National School Outreach](https://www.weather.gov/epz/education)
- Some NWS offices take student volunteers. — [NWS Bismarck Student Volunteers](https://www.weather.gov/bis/StudentVolunteer)
- Through the Citizen Weather Observer Program (CWOP), owners of private automatic stations, schools included, can share their data with NOAA/NWS. — [Davis Instruments: 6 ways to share data](https://www.davisinstruments.com/blogs/newsletter/6-ways-you-can-contribute-to-the-meteorological-community)

**Named US school example**
- South River High School (New Jersey): the Climate Change Club runs a weather station (Columbia Weather Capricorn FLX) on a school building, reports hourly readings to the National Weather Service, and planned for students to learn METAR (aviation weather) code and keep long-term climate records. — [Columbia Weather Systems: Education Climate Club](https://columbiaweather.com/applications/education-climate-club/) [manufacturer case study, about 2 years old; VERIFY current status]
- Historical: in the 1990s the Automated Weather Source (AWS) "WeatherNet" put stations in schools, shared the data with TV broadcasters and the NWS, and built it into classroom curricula. — [Wikipedia: Automated Weather Source](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Automated_Weather_Source)

### Inferences
- The GLOBE approach (a fixed daily time, a standard protocol, upload to a global database, and checking against satellites) can be copied with a thermometer, a rain gauge and a phone running GLOBE Observer, which needs no GLOBE ID for clouds. This makes it realistic for a Zimbabwean school.
- CoCoRaHS gives the strongest, NWS-sourced evidence that simple manual school rain gauges feed real flood warnings.

### Gaps
- Zimbabwe's current GLOBE country status was not found. Check the GLOBE country list at globe.gov.
- CoCoRaHS operates only in the US, Canada, the Bahamas and a few other countries (background knowledge, not verified this session). A Zimbabwean school cannot report to it, but can copy its method.
- The standard CoCoRaHS reading time (about 7 a.m. local) and its founding story (Colorado Climate Center, after the 1997 Fort Collins flood) come from background knowledge and were not verified this session.
- No second strong, current named US school example was found.

## 2. United Kingdom: Met Office WOW, RMetS/MetLink guidance, schools, and GCSE/A-level use

### Takeaway
UK schools have traditionally shared automatic-station data on the Met Office's WOW site and followed RMetS/MetLink guidance on siting. The Met Office is retiring WOW in 2026 with no direct UK replacement, so the film should describe WOW in the past tense or as "being retired."

### Cited Findings
- WOW is "an observations sharing platform, where weather enthusiasts, schools and organisations can all upload and share their latest weather observations." It also shows official Met Office station data, so schools can compare their own readings. — [WOW FAQ (archived copy)](https://tnaqa.mirrorweb.com/ukgwa/20260702044353mp_/https://wow.metoffice.gov.uk/support/faq); [WOW FAQ](https://wow.metoffice.gov.uk/support/faq)
- **Major 2026 change:** the Met Office says it "will be retiring the WOW website later this year (2026)." Future public contributions will go through station manufacturers' own services, and owners of automatic stations may be able to keep sharing that way. The Voluntary Climate Network continues, with a new tool for submitting manual climate observations "coming soon." — [Met Office: Changes to the Weather Observation Website](https://weather.metoffice.gov.uk/learn-about/met-office-for-schools/other-content/other-resources/are-you-obsessed-with-the-weather)
- A quoted Met Office blog says the retirement began in January 2026, with full decommissioning in late 2026 and "no direct replacement" for WOW in the UK. — [weewx docs 5.4 (quoting the Met Office)](https://weewx.com/docs/5.4/usersguide/uploading/); [Node-RED forum](https://discourse.nodered.org/t/uk-private-weather-station-locations/100087) [VERIFY the exact dates on the Met Office page]
- Belgium's RMI separated WOW-BE from the Met Office on 31 December 2025 and relaunched it, working with the Dutch KNMI on an open platform. — [WOW-BE: Why reboot?](https://wow.meteo.be/en/wow-be-reboot-en/why-reboot/)
- In the Met Office primary-school pilot (2015–16), each winning school received an automatic weather station that sent real-time readings to WOW, plus a forecast for its own location that the Met Office said was improved by the school's station. — [Teaching weather and climate science in primary schools (pilot)](https://worldwidescience.org/topicpages/u/uk+primary+school.html); [Fife Today: Newport pupils and the Met Office](https://fifetoday.co.uk/lifestyle/newport-pupils-throw-caution-to-the-wind-in-met-office-link-up-1235079)
- RMetS guidance "A weather station for your school" (Geoff Jenkins, 2015): "a relatively open site is to be preferred, if this is not available then useful measurements can be made in some location in the grounds of almost all schools." The Society does not recommend particular brands. A basic instrument set costs about £100 + VAT, including a self-assembly screen. — [RMetS: School weather station guide (PDF)](https://www.rmets.org/sites/default/files/2019-02/schoolwxstn.pdf); [MetLink: A Weather Station for your School](https://www.metlink.org/fieldwork-resource/a-weather-station-for-your-school/)
- RMetS also publishes a "Guide to Setting up a School Weather Station" and a "Detailed Weather Station Guide." Its automatic weather station guidance warns that "an incorrect exposure for instruments can often invalidate the measurements." — [RMetS Guidelines for Observing](https://www.rmets.org/guidelines-observing); [RMetS AWS site manager (PDF)](https://www.rmets.org/sites/default/files/2019-02/aws-site-manager.pdf); [Setting up a weather station (PDF)](https://www.rmets.org/sites/default/files/2019-02/weather-station-setting-up_1.pdf)
- MetLink ran a free borrow-an-instrument scheme: schools pay postage and are responsible for what they borrow. — [WJEC summary](https://www.wjec.co.uk/media/3b5gi4j5/royal-meteorological-society-borrow-an-instrument-scheme.pdf)
- Curriculum: the National Curriculum for England requires weather to be studied in geography and measured in science. — [MetLink: A Weather Station for your School](https://www.metlink.org/fieldwork-resource/a-weather-station-for-your-school/)
- **Named UK school:** Didcot Girls' School (Oxfordshire) shares its weather station data on WOW, and students use handheld instruments for microclimate investigations in the school grounds. — [MetLink: A Weather Station for your School](https://www.metlink.org/fieldwork-resource/a-weather-station-for-your-school/)
- Newport (Fife) primary pupils worked with the Met Office as part of the schools station pilot. — [Fife Today](https://fifetoday.co.uk/lifestyle/newport-pupils-throw-caution-to-the-wind-in-met-office-link-up-1235079) [details not read in full]
- Fieldwork use: MetLink's microclimate teacher notes suggest using the school weather station as a "control station" for student microclimate surveys, to spot irregular readings and faulty equipment. MetLink also has resources on USB temperature dataloggers and a Met Office worksheet that correlates weather with classroom behaviour. — [MetLink: Microclimates](https://www.metlink.org/topic/microclimates/); [MetLink: Weather Stations](https://www.metlink.org/fieldwork-resource/weather-stations/); [MetLink: Observations and Data](https://www.metlink.org/fieldwork-resource/observations-and-data/)
- The RMetS/Met Office "MetMark" award for weather teaching is paused: "we will not be awarding any MetMarks from 2024." — [MetLink: MetMark](https://www.metlink.org/metmark/)

### Inferences
- The UK example fits the film best as a method: siting, daily readings, comparison with an official station, and microclimate fieldwork with the school station as the control. The UK lesson for Zimbabwe is the same as the UK's own problem: open data platforms can close, so the school should keep its own paper or spreadsheet logbook.
- The microclimate "control station" design maps directly onto a ZIMSEC O-Level Geography school-based project.

### Gaps
- Exact RMetS siting figures were not confirmed this session because the PDF could not be opened. Commonly cited WMO/Met Office standards, from background knowledge: thermometer screen at about 1.25–2 m above short grass; rain gauge rim about 30 cm above ground; a distance from obstacles of at least twice their height (some guides say four times); daily climatological reading at 0900 GMT. [VERIFY before quoting]
- No GCSE or A-level exam-board document naming weather stations was retrieved. The curriculum link above is RMetS's statement about the National Curriculum, not about GCSE specifications.

## 3. Why student weather data matters: farming, early warning, climate records, citizen science, community

### Takeaway
Documented uses include NWS flood warnings and drought assessment (CoCoRaHS), satellite cloud validation (GLOBE), and, in Africa, local data for smallholder farmers (TAHMO, which hosts most of its stations at schools). In Zimbabwe itself, NGO-supported school weather stations in Chimanimani and Chipinge are part of disaster-risk-reduction clubs.

### Cited Findings
- Early warning: CoCoRaHS reports have prompted NWS flood and flash-flood warnings and help estimate soil saturation. — [NWS Pendleton](https://weather.gov/pdt/cocorahs); [NWS Miami](https://weather.gov/mfl/CoCoRaHS)
- Drought and climate records: rainfall records over long periods help pinpoint the areas most affected by drought, and the data is used to study how rainfall varies. — [NWS Jackson CoCoRaHS](https://www.weather.gov/jan/cocorahs/); [NIMSS multistate report](https://nimss.org/seas/33141)
- Satellite science: GLOBE cloud reports are matched with satellite overpasses, and students receive a NASA comparison email. — [MyNASAData GLOBE Connections](https://mynasadata.larc.nasa.gov/globe-connections/globe-connections-air-quality)
- **African school model (TAHMO, the Trans-African Hydro-Meteorological Observatory):** founded in 2014 by John Selker (Oregon State) and Nick van de Giesen (TU Delft), with the aim of 20,000 stations across sub-Saharan Africa. About 700 research-grade stations have been installed. "The majority of weather stations are being installed at local schools where teachers are using the data in their classroom lessons." Its School2School programme has students read daily rainfall and temperature from manual instruments next to the automatic station. The data is aimed at smallholder farmers, climate monitoring and hydrology. — [METER Group: 10 years of TAHMO](https://metergroup.com/case-studies/10-years-of-weather-network-building-in-africa/); [Oregon State Terra](https://terra.oregonstate.edu/?p=19220); [Sidley pro bono: TAHMO](https://www.sidley.com/en/probonolanding/sidley-emerging-enterprises-pro-bono-program/projects/tahmo); [Dietterich 2020 TAHMO overview (PDF)](https://web.engr.oregonstate.edu/~tgd/talks/dietterich-dsa-2020-tahmo-overview.pdf) [station counts vary by date]
- **Zimbabwe example:** the EU-funded Terre des Hommes Italy programme runs DRR clubs in about 40 schools in Chimanimani and Chipinge, and "in 20 of the 40 schools we're working in, we have set up a manual weather station." One named school is Birriiri High School. TDH says the stations were installed with support from the national meteorological service. — [PreventionWeb: Meet high school activists fighting climate change in Zimbabwe](https://www.preventionweb.net/news/meet-high-school-activists-fighting-climate-change-zimbabwe); [The Herald: High school activists fight climate change in Zim](https://www.heraldonline.co.zw/high-school-activists-fight-climate-change-in-zim/); [Terre des Hommes Italy in Zimbabwe](https://terredeshommes.it/eng/?p=1675) [article date not confirmed; the search tool's suggestion that student data feeds a national disaster database could NOT be confirmed]
- A 2026 Save the Children story describes a 12-year-old DRR club member in Chipinge using a rainfall-measuring vessel. — [Save the Children Zimbabwe](https://www.savethechildren.net/zimbabwe/news/strengthening-school-disaster-preparedness-education-continuity-during-crises)
- Zimbabwe's official observation gap: according to the MSD (as reported by PreventionWeb), only about a quarter of the country's roughly 1,400 weather stations were working, and the MSD budget had been cut 25% from 2014 levels. Weather stations in rural schools and clinics in drought-prone areas had once supplied local data. — [PreventionWeb: As official data dries up, Zimbabwe's farmers gauge rainfall themselves](https://www.preventionweb.net/news/official-data-dries-zimbabwes-farmers-gauge-rainfall-themselves) [dated, likely about 2015; VERIFY]
- A Ministry of Primary and Secondary Education "Disaster Risk Management and Resilience in Schools" manual (hosted by UNICEF) is written for School Development Committees, heads, teachers, learners and the community. — [UNICEF Zimbabwe](https://www.unicef.org/zimbabwe/reports/disaster-risk-management-and-resilience-schools-manual)
- 18 UNDP-funded automatic weather stations (Campbell Scientific) were installed in Binga, Chipinge, Chivi and Insiza, with training for the MSD. These are not school-based. — [Campbell Scientific case study (PDF)](https://s.campbellsci.com/documents/au/case-studies/zimbabwe-enhancing-climate-resilience.pdf)

### Inferences
- Because many official stations are not working, a school station's daily rainfall and temperature log could fill a real local data gap for farmers deciding when to plant (for example, judging when cumulative rain makes planting safe). This is an inference, not a documented Zimbabwean outcome.
- Strong lines for the film, all supported by the sources above: "The same method US students use to help NWS flood warnings" (CoCoRaHS); "Schools across Africa already host research weather stations" (TAHMO); "Zimbabwean schools in Chipinge and Chimanimani already run manual weather stations for disaster preparedness" (TDH).

### Gaps
- No source confirmed a current MSD partnership programme for school weather stations in Zimbabwe.
- No documented case was found of Zimbabwean school data being used by local farmers.

## 4. Zimbabwe Education 5.0 and Heritage-Based Education: definition, sources, and fit with a school weather station

### Takeaway
Education 5.0 was announced in 2017 by Prof. Amon Murwira, then Minister of Higher and Tertiary Education, Innovation, Science and Technology Development. It adds innovation and industrialisation to the colonial-era "Education 3.0" missions of teaching, research and community service. It rests on a heritage-based philosophy, and Murwira explicitly lists climate as part of national heritage. In 2024, Cabinet approved the Heritage-Based Education 2024–2030 curriculum framework for schools under the Ministry of Primary and Secondary Education (Minister Torerai Moyo). It was rolled out from 2024 and becomes compulsory for all schools by 2027.

### Cited Findings
- Definition: Murwira described Education 5.0 as education designed to produce graduates able to produce goods and services, going beyond "teaching, research, community service" to add "innovation [and] industrialisation." He called the earlier Education 3.0 (teaching, research and community service) "a colonial design." — [The Standard: Minister defends heritage-based curriculum](https://thestandard.co.zw/local-news/article/200026068/minister-defends-heritage-based-curriculum); [NewsDay (same article)](https://newsday.co.zw/local-news/article/200026068/minister-defends-heritage-based-curriculum)
- The Herald describes a five-mission model of "teaching, research, community outreach, innovation and industrialisation, which is codenamed Education 5.0," with heritage as its underlying philosophy: countries develop by applying knowledge and skill to their heritage, meaning animals, plants, water and minerals. — [The Herald: Four decades on: Curing our education](https://www.heraldonline.co.zw/four-decades-on-curing-our-education/)
- In Parliament, Murwira defined heritage as the country's endowments, listing minerals, water, plants, animals **and climate**. — [Parliament of Zimbabwe Hansard](https://parlzim.gov.zw/?p=19911) [snippet only; VERIFY exact wording]. This is the key hook for a weather station film.
- Origin and dates: Education 5.0 was announced in 2017 by Prof. Amon Murwira, Minister of Higher and Tertiary Education, as part of the National Vision 2030 agenda. — [Frontiers in Education 2025 (PDF)](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/education/articles/10.3389/feduc.2025.1634459/pdf); [ZOU journal article](https://ojs.zou.ac.zw/index.php/iec/article/download/162/131/403)
- Rationale: Murwira cited a literacy rate of 94% against a skills level of only 38%. Innovation hubs are meant to transfer knowledge between researchers, industry and government. — [The Herald: Let's shift from analysis to creation (2019)](https://www.pressreader.com/zimbabwe/the-herald-zimbabwe/20190302/281543702221854); [The Herald: Varsities urged to embrace innovation](https://www.heraldonline.co.zw/varsities-urged-to-embrace-innovation/)
- University of Zimbabwe: the framework is driven by a heritage-based philosophy and adds the two new missions to the existing three. — [University of Zimbabwe: About](https://www.uz.ac.zw/index.php/49-about-uz)
- Academic paper: the curriculum is "anchored on five pillars: Teaching, Research, Community engagement, Innovation and Industrialization." — [IJRAW 2024: Implementation of Heritage-Based Education 5.0 in tertiary institutions](https://academicjournal.ijraw.com/media/post/IJRAW-3-8-18.1.pdf)

- **Extension to schools:** Cabinet approved the Heritage-Based Education 2024–2030 curriculum framework. Reports say it was announced in February 2024 and rolled out from May 2024, covering early childhood to upper secondary, except exam classes. Grade 7, Form 4 and Form 6 learners continued on the 2015–2022 framework for their 2025 exams. — [The Herald: Cabinet okays Heritage-Based framework](https://www.heraldonline.co.zw/cabinet-okays-heritage-based-framework/); [NewsDay: New curriculum divides teachers](https://www.newsday.co.zw/local-news/article/200023934/new-curriculum-divides-teachers); [The Herald: Understanding the new heritage-based curriculum](https://www.heraldonline.co.zw/understanding-the-new-heritage-based-curriculum-2/)
- Structure: there are two learner pathways (academic and vocational). Subjects were cut from about 11 to 6 in primary and 7 to 5 in secondary, with five compulsory O-Level subjects including Heritage Studies, according to a union leader. Continuous assessment was replaced by school-based projects. — [The Independent: Chaos in schools over new curriculum](https://theindependent.co.zw/local-news/article/200026921/chaos-in-schools-over-new-curriculum); [CITE](https://cite.org.zw/?p=40780) [union figures; VERIFY against MoPSE]
- Minister Torerai Moyo said the curriculum emphasises indigenous languages, history, technical subjects and vocational training to support industrialisation and innovation, and builds "critical thinking skills and problem-solving skills." Innovation hubs have been set up in schools across the provinces, including three in Victoria Falls. Over 500 teachers have been trained in coding and robotics. — [Bulawayo24: Moyo announces rollout](https://bulawayo24.com/index-id-news-sc-national-byo-264618.html); [Zimbabwe Situation: Heritage-based curriculum now compulsory](https://www.zimbabwesituation.com/news/heritage-based-curriculum-now-compulsory/); [The Herald: Schools ordered to adopt heritage-based curriculum or face closure](https://www.heraldonline.co.zw/schools-ordered-to-adopt-heritage-based-curriculum-or-face-closure/)

### How a school weather station demonstrates each pillar (synthesis for the film)
Inference, built on the definitions above:
- **Heritage (foundation):** climate is named as national heritage, alongside water, plants, animals and minerals ([Parliament](https://parlzim.gov.zw/?p=19911)). Learners can record local weather alongside indigenous weather knowledge (signs such as tree flowering, bird behaviour and wind direction) and compare the two.
- **Teaching:** a daily routine of reading max/min temperature, rainfall, cloud type and cover, and wind, following the GLOBE or RMetS method, supports Geography (weather and climate), Science, Mathematics (averages, graphs) and Heritage Studies.
