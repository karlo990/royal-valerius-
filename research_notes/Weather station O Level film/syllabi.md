## Q0. Which syllabus versions are current? (This sets the scope for everything else.)

### Takeaway
**This is a key finding for the film.** Cambridge 2217 is changing. The syllabus for exams up to and including **2026** has a dedicated "2.4 Weather" topic with weather-station instruments. The **new 2027–2029 syllabus (first exam November 2027) appears to drop the standalone Weather topic** in favour of "Climate change". ZIMSEC O Level Geography is still code **4022** under the 2024–2030 Heritage-Based Curriculum (HBC), with 2026 specimen papers. I found no evidence of a "4037" code for O Level Geography.

### Cited Findings
- Cambridge publishes a separate 2217 syllabus "for exams in 2026", and for 2026 "there are no significant changes which affect teaching". — [Cambridge 2217 2026 syllabus](https://www.cambridgeinternational.org/Images/697292-2026-syllabus.pdf); [Cambridge 2217 qualification page](https://www.cambridgeinternational.org/programmes-and-qualifications/cambridge-o-level-geography-2217/)

The separate Geographical Skills paper is removed and skills are "assessed across all question papers". ### Inferences
- A film made in 2026 for Zimbabwean students reaches two Cambridge cohorts. **November 2026 candidates** still sit the weather-station content. **2027+ candidates** (who started 2217 in 2025/26) are likely not examined on instruments directly, only on climate, climate graphs and climate change. The film should present weather-station content as core for ZIMSEC and for Cambridge up to 2026, and as background/skills for Cambridge 2027+.

## Q1. Which weather instruments are named, and what must students know (siting, use, reading, recording)?

### Takeaway
Cambridge (up to 2026) names eight instruments plus the Stevenson screen and cloud observation. Students must know each one's **characteristics, siting and use**. ZIMSEC requires the Stevenson screen, factors governing where a weather station is sited, measuring and recording weather data, and **running a functional school weather station**.

### Cited Findings
**Cambridge (2.4 Weather, editions to 2026, shared with IGCSE 0460):**
- Learning outcomes: "describe how weather data are collected", "make calculations using information from weather instruments", "use and interpret graphs and other diagrams showing weather and climate data". Guidance covers the characteristics, siting and use of a **Stevenson Screen, rain gauge, maximum–minimum thermometer, wet-and-dry bulb thermometer (hygrometer), sunshine recorder, barometer, anemometer and wind vane**, plus "observations of types and amounts of cloud". — [Study Mind 2.4 Weather](https://studymind.co.uk/notes/2-4-weather/); [Cambridge IGCSE 0460 2025–2026 syllabus](https://www.cambridgeinternational.org/Images/664610-2025-2026-syllabus.pdf); [0460 2020–2022 syllabus](https://www.cambridgeinternational.org/Images/414139-2020-2022-syllabus.pdf)
- Older Cambridge wording: "describe and explain the characteristics, siting and use made of a Stevenson screen". — search extract of [0460 syllabus (English School Cyprus copy)](https://www.englishschool.ac.cy/udata/contents/files/docs/Departments/Geography/igcse-cambridge-years-4-5.pdf)
- Stevenson screen features: a raised wooden box, painted white, with slatted/louvred sides and a double-layered roof, sited over grass. Readings are taken at the same time each day. — [SME 2217 Collecting weather data](https://www.savemyexams.com/o-level/geography/cie/22/revision-notes/2-the-natural-environment/2-4-weather/2-4-1-collecting-weather-data/)
- Stevenson screen legs about 121 cm, with thermometer bulbs around 125 cm. — [SME IGCSE weather instruments](https://www.savemyexams.com/igcse/geography/cie/20/revision-notes/2-the-natural-environment/2-4-weather/2-4-1-collecting-weather-data/); [IGCSE Unit 4 scheme of work](https://www.theallpapers.com/papers/CIE/IGCSE/Geography%20(0460)/0460_nos_sw_4.pdf)
- The screen gives a standardised environment for temperature and humidity (and historically pressure) measurement. — [Wikipedia: Stevenson screen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stevenson_screen)
- Maximum–minimum thermometer = **Six's thermometer**, housed in the screen. Daily range = max − min. — [SME 2217 Collecting weather data](https://www.savemyexams.com/o-level/geography/cie/22/revision-notes/2-the-natural-environment/2-4-weather/2-4-1-collecting-weather-data/)
- Reading convention for a Six's thermometer: the left-hand limb reads the minimum and the right-hand limb reads the maximum (readings taken at the bottom of the index/marker). — [KIS IGCSE Geography](https://kisigcsegeography.wordpress.com/unit-2-3-weather-and-climate/)
- Rain gauge sited in the open, away from buildings and trees. One rule given: distance from the nearest obstacle should be at least twice that object's height. — [SME IGCSE weather instruments](https://www.savemyexams.com/igcse/geography/cie/20/revision-notes/2-the-natural-environment/2-4-weather/2-4-1-collecting-weather-data/); [Andrew's Geography revision](http://andrewsgeographyrevision.blogspot.com/2016/07/describe-how-weather-data-is-collected.html)
- Anemometer: rotating cups on a vertical shaft measure wind speed (units m/s or km/h). Wind vane shows the direction the wind comes **from**. Both are placed high and clear of obstacles. — [SME 2217 Collecting weather data](https://www.savemyexams.com/o-level/geography/cie/22/revision-notes/2-the-natural-environment/2-4-weather/2-4-1-collecting-weather-data/); [SME IGCSE weather instruments](https://www.savemyexams.com/igcse/geography/cie/20/revision-notes/2-the-natural-environment/2-4-weather/2-4-1-collecting-weather-data/)
- Further teacher resources for instrument use: [RMetS MetLink Unit 1 Using weather instruments](https://www.metlink.org/wp-content/uploads/2020/11/Unit-1-Using-weather-instruments-TEACHER.pdf); [Tes 2.4 Weather resource](https://www.tes.com/teaching-resource/cambridge-igcse-geography-2-4-weather-13423324)

**ZIMSEC:**
- The 2015 ZIMSEC Forms 1–4 Geography syllabus lists, under weather/climate: the **Stevenson screen**, **factors governing the siting of a weather station**, and **measuring and recording weather data**. It also expects schools to **set up and run a functional weather station**. — [Studocu copy of ZIMSEC Geography Syllabus Forms 1–4 (2015)](https://www.studocu.com/row/document/mutare-polytechnic-college/diesel-plant-fitting/geography-syllabus-for-forms-1-4-zimsec-2015-edition/135630021)
- The Ministry Forms 1–6 Geography document lists weather instruments, school weather stations, and reading and recording weather data, with cross-cutting themes that include heritage, climate change and technology. A Form 3 "school weather station" entry appears in its scope and sequence. — [MoPSE Geography Forms 1–6 (zimsake mirror)](https://www.zimsake.co.zw/softidrive/download/7/sh/be8e9bc5bb4479b288142270f7168226)
- One school scheme places the Stevenson screen, weather statistics and air masses in **Form 3**, and climate in **Form 4**. — [Scribd Forms 1–4 scheme](https://www.scribd.com/document/812378878/Form-1-scheme-Geo)
- Zimbabwean revision notes: the Stevenson screen houses the Six's thermometer and is painted white to reflect heat. The weather station topic covers temperature, humidity, pressure and wind. — [revision.co.zw Geography O Level notes](https://revision.co.zw/notes/ordinary-level-notes/geography-o-level-notes/)
- A "new curriculum" 4022 note set lists Form 3 Topic 1 as **Weather**, with sub-topics including weather statistics, precipitation and types, weather hazards and human influence on weather. Form 4 covers climate of Zimbabwe, climate variability, climatic types and climate change. — [Studocu Geo new curr O Level notes (4022)](https://www.studocu.com/row/document/university-of-zimbabwe/geology-for-engineers/geo-new-curr-o-level-notes/65743859); [Studocu 4022 Form 3 notes](https://www.studocu.com/row/document/dadaya-high-school/math/geography-notes-4022-form-3-weather-climate-and-landforms/153185877)
- Stevenson screen description in Form 1 notes: a white wooden box on four legs, louvred sides for free air entry, double roof to keep out the sun's heat. — [ecolebooks Form 1 weather](https://www.ecolebooks.com/geography-form-1-weather/) (East African site; generic)

### Inferences
- Both boards share the same core instrument set. On-screen siting rules that serve both: screen over short grass, door facing away from direct sun (south-facing in the southern hemisphere, i.e. Zimbabwe; north in the northern hemisphere), about 1.2 m height, open site, gauge clear of obstacles, anemometer/vane high and exposed, readings at a fixed time daily. *The door-orientation point is standard meteorological practice and was not found in the syllabus extracts.*
- A demonstration of a working school weather station directly matches ZIMSEC's "functional weather station" expectation.

### Gaps
- I could not see the exact list of instruments in the 4022 HBC syllabus (for example, whether the sunshine recorder and barometer are named). No official HBC 4022 syllabus PDF was found online.
- Exact Cambridge 2217 wording was confirmed via 0460 and third-party copies only.

## Q2. Which calculations and skills are examined?

### Takeaway
Cambridge (to 2026) examines calculations from instrument data: **daily/diurnal range, mean daily and mean monthly temperature, annual range, total rainfall, relative humidity from wet/dry bulb readings and tables**, and **drawing and interpreting climate graphs and wind roses**. ZIMSEC notes stress "weather statistics" and the use of hygrometer tables. I found no evidence that synoptic chart interpretation is required at O Level in either board.

### Cited Findings
- Cambridge outcome: "make calculations using information from weather instruments"; "use and interpret graphs and other diagrams showing weather and climate data". — [Study Mind 2.4](https://studymind.co.uk/notes/2-4-weather/); [0460 2025–2026 syllabus](https://www.cambridgeinternational.org/Images/664610-2025-2026-syllabus.pdf)
- 2217 revision note "Weather data" covers rainfall (mm, plotted as bars), diurnal temperature range, mean daily temperature, mean monthly temperature, annual range, wind data, and descriptive bands for rainfall and temperature ranges. — [SME 2217 Weather data](https://www.savemyexams.com/o-level/geography/cie/22/revision-notes/2-the-natural-environment/2-4-weather/2-4-2-weather-data/)
- 2217 "Weather data interpretation": climate graph with temperature as a line and precipitation as bars. Describe the pattern, identify the wettest/driest seasons, and calculate range = highest − lowest. — [SME 2217 Weather data interpretation](https://www.savemyexams.com/o-level/geography/cie/22/revision-notes/2-the-natural-environment/2-4-weather/2-4-3-weather-data-interpretation/)
- Definitions: mean daily temperature = (max + min) ÷ 2. Annual range = highest mean monthly − lowest mean monthly. — [highmarks.io KCSE weather elements](https://highmarks.io/kcse-revision/geography/weather-elements) (generic, non-board source)
- Relative humidity from a hygrometer: the larger the wet/dry difference, the lower the humidity. Equal readings = saturated (100%). RH is read from a table using the dry-bulb temperature and the depression of the wet bulb. — [pdfcoffee ZIMSEC O Level Geography notes](https://pdfcoffee.com/zimsec-o-level-geography-notes-pdf-pdf-free.html); [WikiEducator Weather and Climate](https://wikieducator.org/GHS/Geography/Weather_and_Climate)
- Wind rose: bars on a compass show how often the wind blows from each direction over a period. — [Lets Master Everything: Weather and Climate](https://www.letsmastereverythingsimple.com/2026/09/weather-and-climate-detailed-summary.html) (generic)
- Cambridge 2217 (2027+): geographical skills (graphs, charts etc.) are assessed across Papers 1 and 2. Save My Exams 2217 (25 edition) has a "Geographical skills > Graphical skills > Charts" section. — [Cambridge 2217 page](https://www.cambridgeinternational.org/programmes-and-qualifications/cambridge-o-level-geography-2217/); [SME 2217 (25) Charts](https://www.savemyexams.com/o-level/geography/cie/25/revision-notes/geographical-skills/graphical-skills/charts/)

### Inferences
- Suggested worked examples for the film, each covering both boards: (a) Six's thermometer reading → daily range and mean; (b) a week of rain-gauge readings → total and mean; (c) wet 18 °C / dry 22 °C → 4 °C depression → look up RH in a table; (d) plot a Harare climate graph (bars + line); (e) wind-vane tallies → wind rose.
- Synoptic charts seem to be beyond scope for both O Levels (no hits in syllabus extracts). Keep them as enrichment only.

### Gaps
- No primary-source confirmation that relative humidity *calculation from tables* is explicitly named in the 2217 text (it is implied by "calculations using information from weather instruments" plus the hygrometer).
- No ZIMSEC 4022 HBC primary text on required calculations. The Zimbabwe MSD humidity table was not found online.

## Q3. Clouds, oktas, cloud formation and types of rainfall

### Takeaway
Cambridge (to 2026) requires "observations of types and amounts of cloud". Teaching materials specify **cirrus, cumulus, cumulonimbus, stratus** and cover measured in **oktas (eighths)**. ZIMSEC teaches precipitation and its types: **convectional, orographic/relief, and cyclonic/frontal/convergence (including ITCZ) rainfall**. In Zimbabwe, ITCZ/convectional storms are linked to cumulonimbus.

### Cited Findings
- Cambridge 0460/2217 guidance: "observations of types and amounts of cloud". — [0460 2025–2026 syllabus](https://www.cambridgeinternational.org/Images/664610-2025-2026-syllabus.pdf); [Study Mind 2.4](https://studymind.co.uk/notes/2-4-weather/)
- Cambridge teaching guide (scheme of work) lists cloud types **cirrus, stratus, cumulus, cumulonimbus**, with cover in eighths. Older syllabus wording: main cloud types and estimating cloud cover. — [Cambridge IGCSE 0460 scheme of work Unit 4](https://www.theallpapers.com/papers/CIE/IGCSE/Geography%20(0460)/0460_nos_sw_4.pdf)
- Okta = one-eighth of the sky covered by cloud. — [SME IGCSE weather instruments](https://www.savemyexams.com/igcse/geography/cie/20/revision-notes/2-the-natural-environment/2-4-weather/2-4-1-collecting-weather-data/); [Quizlet IGCSE weather](https://quizlet.com/sg/182504716/igcse-weather-and-instruments-flash-cards/)
- ZIMSEC O Level outline (revision notes) covers rainfall types: orographic, convectional, and cyclonic/frontal/convergence including ITCZ. — [revision.co.zw](https://revision.co.zw/notes/ordinary-level-notes/geography-o-level-notes/)
- ITCZ convergence and heating force moist air to rise, cool and condense into towering **cumulonimbus**, giving torrential rain with thunder and lightning. ITCZ air masses are moist and RH is high. — [pdfcoffee ZIMSEC notes](https://pdfcoffee.com/zimsec-o-level-geography-notes-pdf-pdf-free.html); [revision.co.zw](https://revision.co.zw/notes/ordinary-level-notes/geography-o-level-notes/)
- 4022 Form 3 notes list "Precipitation and types" as a Weather sub-topic. — [Studocu 4022 notes](https://www.studocu.com/row/document/university-of-zimbabwe/geology-for-engineers/geo-new-curr-o-level-notes/65743859)
- Cambridge 2217 "Climate characteristics" (2.5, to 2026) covers equatorial and hot-desert climates (convectional rain context). — [SME 2217 Climate characteristics](https://www.savemyexams.com/o-level/geography/cie/22/revision-notes/2-the-natural-environment/2-5-climate-and-natural-vegetation/2-5-1-climate-characteristics/)

### Inferences
- The set to film: cirrus (high, wispy), cumulus (heaped, fair weather), cumulonimbus (Zimbabwe summer thunderstorms), stratus (low grey layer, e.g. "guti" drizzle in eastern Zimbabwe; *the guti link is my inference and needs checking with a Zimbabwean source*). Nimbostratus is not named in any extract, so include it only as optional extra.
- Types of rainfall are **explicitly ZIMSEC** content. In Cambridge (to 2026) they come in indirectly through climate (convectional rain in equatorial regions). I found no extract naming relief/frontal rainfall in 2217.

### Gaps
- I did not find the exact 2217 syllabus wording on cloud types, or confirmation that cloud formation processes (adiabatic cooling, dew point) are examined at 2217.
- ZIMSEC HBC primary wording on clouds and oktas was not found.

## Q4. Zimbabwe-specific weather and climate content (ZIMSEC)

### Takeaway
ZIMSEC 4022 includes **Climate of Zimbabwe**, climate variability and climate change (Form 4), plus weather hazards and human influence on weather (Form 3). Heritage-Based Curriculum themes (heritage, sustainability, climate change) cut across the syllabus. Real Zimbabwe data (rainy season, ITCZ, El Niño droughts, MSD forecasts) provide strong local case material.

### Cited Findings
- 4022 notes: Form 4 sub-topics "Climate of Zimbabwe", "climate variability", "climatic types", "climate change". Form 3: "weather hazards", "human influence on weather". — [Studocu 4022 new curriculum notes](https://www.studocu.com/row/document/university-of-zimbabwe/geology-for-engineers/geo-new-curr-o-level-notes/65743859)
- The MoPSE Geography document lists "atmospheric processes and phenomena" as a learning area and has cross-cutting themes including heritage and climate change. — [MoPSE Geography Forms 1–6](https://www.zimsake.co.zw/softidrive/download/7/sh/be8e9bc5bb4479b288142270f7168226)
- 2026 HBC specimen Paper 1 (multiple choice) uses a 1:50,000 Masvingo survey map with questions on heritage sites. The Paper 2 marking scheme covers wetlands and mining in Zimbabwe. These are heritage/sustainability-led, and I found no weather item reported. — [Sytbay](https://sytbay.co.zw/download/zimsec-o-level-geography-specimen-papers-4022-1-4022-2-2026-hbc-pdf/); [Scribd 2026 marking scheme](https://www.scribd.com/document/1048935915/Geo-2026-Marking-Scheme-Specimen-Corrections-Are-Permitted)
- Seasons: warm rainy season about **November–March** (some sources say October–March), cool dry winter May–August, hot dry season August–October. Rain increases when the ITCZ shifts further south. — [GlobalSecurity Zimbabwe climate](https://www.globalsecurity.org/military/world/africa/zw-climate.htm) (republished older text, undated); [World Bank Climate Knowledge Portal](https://climateknowledgeportal.worldbank.org/node/4526)
- National mean annual rainfall is about **670 mm** (1991–2020). About two-thirds of the country gets under 800 mm, and parts of the Eastern Highlands exceed 1,200 mm. — [World Bank CKP](https://climateknowledgeportal.worldbank.org/node/4526); [GlobalSecurity](https://www.globalsecurity.org/military/world/africa/zw-climate.htm)
- ENSO: El Niño → below-average rain. 2015–16 El Niño gave about 75% of typical rainfall, with south/west under 100 mm against a normal 450–650 mm. The 2023–24 MSD forecast was normal-to-below-normal, the rains came about five weeks late, and the government advised farmers to stop planting in January. — [World Bank CKP](https://climateknowledgeportal.worldbank.org/node/4526); [ReliefWeb El Niño AA plan](https://reliefweb.int/report/zimbabwe/zimbabwe-el-nino-anticipatory-action-plan-oct-2023-mar-2024-issued-november-2023); [FEWS NET Nov 2023](https://fews.net/southern-africa/zimbabwe/key-message-update/november-2023); [Capital FM](https://capitalfm.africa/zimbabwean-government-advises-farmers-to-stop-planting-amid-drought-fears/)
- The November–March season may be shortening (1980–2013 analysis). — [South African Journal of Science](https://sajs.co.za/article/view/6517/10306)
- Average relative humidity peaks around 77% in February and falls to about 45% in September. — [Wikipedia: Geography of Zimbabwe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Geography_of_Zimbabwe)

### Inferences
- Film hooks: an MSD seasonal forecast, comparing a February (humid, cumulonimbus) reading with a September (dry, clear) reading, and El Niño drought impacts on farmers. Together these meet the HBC emphasis on local heritage and lived experience.
- Possible indigenous knowledge links (traditional weather signs) fit HBC's heritage theme, but I found **no source** showing that ZIMSEC requires them.

### Gaps
- I found no official HBC 4022 syllabus text naming the MSD, the ITCZ or indigenous weather knowledge.
- No information was found on the HBC continuous assessment / project component (e.g. a weather-station project) or its weighting.

## Q5. Example exam question styles

### Takeaway
Full past papers could not be opened. The evidence shows that ZIMSEC Paper 2 has had weather-station questions (e.g. June 2019). Cambridge (to 2026) asked weather questions with figures in Paper 1 (2.4) and in Paper 4 (alternative to coursework, often weather fieldwork). Typical styles are described below.

### Cited Findings
