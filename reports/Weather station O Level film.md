# Turn Royal Crest's Weather Station Into a Classroom

The film's core should be a correctly sited school weather station filmed through a Bikita summer day: morning readings at a fixed time, then afternoon thermals, towering cumulus, a cumulonimbus thunderstorm, and finally reading the rain gauge. That one storyline covers what both exam boards test. Each instrument has a standard setup. The Stevenson screen is white and louvred, its thermometer bulbs sit 1.25–2 m above the ground, and in Zimbabwe its door faces **south**. The rain gauge rim sits 30 cm above the ground. The anemometer and wind vane go high and in the open, and wind is always named for the direction it blows **from**. Zimbabwe's rain is **about 90% convective**, so a cumulonimbus storm is the honest local example. Nimbostratus belongs to cloud-band days or the Eastern Highlands. The biggest issue for the script is timing. **Cambridge 2217 examines weather instruments only up to the November 2026 series.** The 2027–2029 syllabus appears to drop the standalone Weather topic in favour of Climate change. **ZIMSEC 4022** under the Heritage-Based Curriculum still expects schools to run a working weather station. US and UK school networks, and African programmes such as TAHMO, show that student readings feed real flood warnings, satellite checks and advice to farmers. Zimbabwe's Education 5.0 counts climate as national heritage. The careers branch leads to UZ's BSc Hons Meteorology & Climate Science, to GIS, geography, water, agriculture and disaster degrees, and to employers including the Meteorological Services Department (MSD). One caveat applies throughout: most sources were read as search extracts rather than full pages, so exact syllabus wording and programme titles must be checked before they appear on screen.

## Eight instruments, one discipline: standard exposure makes readings comparable

The reason for siting rules is the best first line of narration: readings only mean something if every station measures the same way. The Stevenson screen is "always mounted on a stand at such a height that the thermometer bulbs are between 1.25 m and 2 m above ground level" so that stations can be compared ([Environment Canada MANCLIM](https://www.canada.ca/en/environment-climate-change/services/weather-manuals-documentation/manclim-climatological-observations/temperature.html)). Revision materials aimed at the exam give legs of about 121 cm and bulbs at about 125 cm ([Save My Exams](https://www.savemyexams.com/igcse/geography/cie/20/revision-notes/2-the-natural-environment/2-4-weather/2-4-1-collecting-weather-data/)). Each feature has a purpose. White paint reflects solar radiation, and louvres let air flow so the thermometers measure air temperature rather than sunshine ([WeatherOnline](https://www.weatheronline.pl/reports/wxfacts/The-Stevenson-Screen.htm)). The double roof keeps out the sun's heat, and the screen stands over grass ([Save My Exams 2217](https://www.savemyexams.com/o-level/geography/cie/22/revision-notes/2-the-natural-environment/2-4-weather/2-4-1-collecting-weather-data/)). The detail most likely to go wrong in a Zimbabwean film is the door. It faces north in the Northern Hemisphere and **south in the Southern Hemisphere**, so the sun does not fall on the thermometers when the door is opened ([Pakistan Meteorological Department](https://metkhi.pmd.gov.pk/stevenson-screen.php); [Weather for Schools](https://weatherforschools.me.uk/html/settingup.html)). Many British and Wikipedia-derived diagrams give only the "face north" rule, and copying them into a film shot in Bikita would be a visible error.

The film should name this as a practical compromise, not present it as the rule. Second, the barometer's indoor placement is standard textbook practice, but no source collected here confirms it. The barometer, the mercury-free Six's thermometer ([Weather for Schools](https://www.weatherforschools.me.uk/html/maxmin.html)) and the "short grass" surface should be checked against the RMetS station guide before narration is locked. That guide also reassures schools that "useful measurements can be made in some location in the grounds of almost all schools" even where a perfect open site is not available ([RMetS](https://www.rmets.org/sites/default/files/2019-02/schoolwxstn.pdf)).

## Ten cloud genera, eight oktas, and why cumulonimbus suits Bikita better than nimbostratus

The WMO classifies clouds into **ten genera, and each observed cloud belongs to exactly one** ([WMO](https://wmo.int/world-meteorological-day-2017/classifying-clouds)). The Latin roots are good teaching material: cirrus means hair or fibre, cumulus means heap, and stratus means layer ([WMO Cloud Atlas](https://cloudatlas.wmo.int/en/cloud-classification-summary.html)). Nimbostratus is a "grey cloud layer, often dark... rendered diffuse by more or less continuously falling rain or snow, which, in most cases, reaches the ground. It is thick enough throughout to blot out the Sun" ([WMO Cloud Atlas: Nimbostratus](https://cloudatlas.wmo.int/en/nimbostratus-ns.html)). It is told apart from altostratus by precipitation actually reaching the ground ([Cloud Atlas](https://cloudatlas.wmo.int/en/ns-compared-with-as-op.html)). Cumulonimbus is the Atlas's thunderstorm cloud. Cloud amount is reported in **oktas, or eighths of the sky**: 0 is clear, 8 is overcast and 9 means the sky is obscured, for example by fog. The Met Office calls 3–4 oktas "scattered" and 5–7 "broken" ([Met Office](https://weather.metoffice.gov.uk/guides/observations/how-we-measure-cloud)).

For the exam, the cloud set to teach is narrower than the atlas. The Cambridge scheme of work names **cirrus, stratus, cumulus and cumulonimbus**, with cover estimated in eighths ([Cambridge 0460 scheme of work](https://www.theallpapers.com/papers/CIE/IGCSE/Geography%20(0460)/0460_nos_sw_4.pdf)). No syllabus extract found names nimbostratus, so the film should include it as a contrast to cumulonimbus, not as core content. That contrast is useful: layered cloud with steady rain compared with towering cloud with short, violent storms. The cloud height bands often quoted for drone or animation sequences (low cloud up to about 2 km, and high cloud reaching about 18 km in the tropics) were not verified in this research. They should be checked against the Cloud Atlas before they are drawn to scale.

## Rising air cools 9.8 °C per kilometre until rain forms, and in Zimbabwe convection does most of the lifting

The physics fits a single animated sequence. A rising parcel of air expands and cools at the **dry adiabatic lapse rate of 9.8 °C/km** until it reaches its dew point, the temperature at which relative humidity is 100%. Above that level, condensation releases latent heat, so the saturated rate is lower, about **4 °C/km at 20 °C** ([Wikipedia: Lapse rate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lapse_rate)). Whether the air keeps rising depends on stability. Air is unstable when the surrounding (environmental) lapse rate is greater than the adiabatic rate, and that is when cumulus grows into cumulonimbus. Cloud droplets become raindrops in two ways. In warm clouds, drops collide and merge (collision–coalescence). In clouds that rise above the freezing level, ice crystals grow at the expense of supercooled droplets, because saturation vapour pressure over ice is lower than over water. This is the Bergeron–Findeisen process ([AMS Glossary](https://glossary.ametsoc.org/wiki/bergeron-findeisen-process/); [College of DuPage](https://weather.cod.edu/sirvatka/bergeron.html)). Three uplift mechanisms produce the three rainfall types that ZIMSEC examines. **Convectional** rain comes from local ground heating and gives showers and thunderstorms. **Relief** rain forms as moist air is forced up a windward slope, leaving a rain shadow on the descending lee side. **Frontal** rain forms where warm air rises over cold air ([MetLink/RMetS](https://www.metlink.org/resource/in-depth-rainfall-and-relief); [The Geo Room, Zimbabwe](https://www.thegeoroom.co.zw/climatology/precipitation.php)). ZIMSEC notes also teach convergence (ITCZ) rainfall, in which the meeting of air streams and surface heating build towering cumulonimbus and torrential rain ([revision.co.zw](https://revision.co.zw/notes/ordinary-level-notes/geography-o-level-notes/)).

The Zimbabwe data makes the choice of storyline clear. About **90% of the country's precipitation is convective**, and the rest comes from orographic and frontal systems ([Finnish Red Cross climate profile](https://www.punainenristi.fi/globalassets/6.-tyomme--paasivu/kansainvalinen-apu/kehitysyhteistyo/ilmastoriskit/climate-profiles-of-countries-in-southern-africa-zimbabwe.pdf)). The rains fall from about November to March, under the ITCZ's influence. When the ITCZ is poorly defined, serious drought is likely ([GlobalSecurity](https://www.globalsecurity.org/military/world/africa/zw-climate.htm)). National mean rainfall is about **670 mm**, and the Eastern Highlands exceed 1,200 mm in places ([World Bank CKP](https://climateknowledgeportal.worldbank.org/node/4526)). Masvingo averages about **625 mm** ([Weather Spark](https://de.weatherspark.com/countries/ZW/08)). In 1988/89, **82% of Masvingo District was Natural Region IV** ([FAO](https://www.fao.org/4/AB967E/AB967E10.htm)), where 450–600 mm falls and seasonal drought is frequent ([FAO](https://www.fao.org/3/X5594E/X5594e03.htm)). Region boundaries are moving with climate variability: Region II has shrunk by 49% ([Mugandani et al. 2012](https://www.ajol.info/index.php/acsj/article/view/81761)). Humidity readings can show the seasons: relative humidity averages about **77% in February and about 45% in September** ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Geography_of_Zimbabwe)). A February hygrometer reading set against a September one makes a ready-made exam calculation. The 2015–16 El Niño, when the south and west received under 100 mm against a normal 450–650 mm ([World Bank CKP](https://climateknowledgeportal.worldbank.org/node/4526)), gives the film its stakes. One point needs care: no source confirmed Bikita's own Natural Region classification, its mean rainfall, or its supposed rain-shadow position east of the Eastern Highlands' moist easterlies. The film should use Masvingo-level figures unless MSD station data for Bikita is obtained.

## Cambridge stops examining instruments after 2026, while ZIMSEC wants a working station

The exam content is shared across the two boards, but the timetable is not. Cambridge 2217 up to the 2026 exams carries a "Weather" topic (2.4). Its learning outcomes are to "describe how weather data are collected", "make calculations using information from weather instruments" and "use and interpret graphs and other diagrams showing weather and climate data". It names the characteristics, siting and use of the Stevenson screen, rain gauge, max–min thermometer, wet-and-dry bulb hygrometer, sunshine recorder, barometer, anemometer and wind vane, plus "observations of types and amounts of cloud" ([Study Mind](https://studymind.co.uk/notes/2-4-weather/); [Cambridge 0460 2025–2026 syllabus](https://www.cambridgeinternational.org/Images/664610-2025-2026-syllabus.pdf)). The new 2217 syllabus for 2027–2029 runs in the November series only. It moves to two papers (Physical and Human Geography, each 75 marks), assesses skills across both papers, and lists physical topics of rivers, coasts, ecosystems, tectonic hazards and **climate change, with no standalone weather topic in the extracts found** ([Cambridge 2217 2027–2029](https://www.cambridgeinternational.org/Images/718204-2027-2029-syllabus.pdf)). This has not yet been checked against the "Changes to this syllabus" page of the PDF. If it holds, the film reaches two Cambridge cohorts: for November 2026 candidates the instruments are core content, and for 2027+ candidates they are background for climate graphs and climate change.

ZIMSEC Geography is still **4022**. Its 2026 specimen papers are 4022/1 and 4022/2 under the 2024–2030 Heritage-Based Curriculum ([Sytbay](https://sytbay.co.zw/download/zimsec-o-level-geography-specimen-papers-4022-1-4022-2-2026-hbc-pdf/)), and no "4037" O Level code was found. The 2015 Forms 1–4 syllabus lists the Stevenson screen, "factors governing the siting of a weather station", measuring and recording weather data, and an expectation that schools **set up and run a functional weather station** ([Studocu copy of ZIMSEC syllabus](https://www.studocu.com/row/document/mutare-polytechnic-college/diesel-plant-fitting/geography-syllabus-for-forms-1-4-zimsec-2015-edition/135630021)). Current notes place Weather (statistics, precipitation types, hazards, human influence) in Form 3 and Climate of Zimbabwe, variability and climate change in Form 4 ([Studocu 4022 notes](https://www.studocu.com/row/document/university-of-zimbabwe/geology-for-engineers/geo-new-curr-o-level-notes/65743859)). The June 2019 Paper 2 included weather-station questions ([Scribd](https://www.scribd.com/document/899813923/523013191-Zimsec-O-Level-Geography-June-2019-Past-Exam-Paper-2-PDF-ELIBRARY)).

## US and UK schools show student readings feed flood warnings and satellite checks

The strongest evidence that school data matters comes from the US. **CoCoRaHS** has schools put out a rain gauge and report at the same time each morning ([NSTA](https://www.nsta.org/science-teacher/science-teacher-february-2020/gauging-rainfall-cocorahs)). National Weather Service offices say these reports help estimate soil saturation and have led forecasters to issue flood and flash-flood warnings. Over longer periods the data identifies the areas worst hit by drought and is used to check radar rainfall estimates ([NWS Pendleton](https://weather.gov/pdt/cocorahs); [NWS Jackson](https://www.weather.gov/jan/cocorahs/)). **GLOBE**, run by NASA and the NSF since 1995 ([NASA](https://www.nasa.gov/press-release/globe-program-marks-20-years-of-global-earth-science-education)), has involved more than 24,000 schools ([USF C-IMAGE](https://www.marine.usf.edu/c-image/globe-training/)). Students read precipitation and max/min temperature daily within an hour of solar noon. Their cloud reports are matched to satellite overpasses, because "satellites only see the top of the clouds while you see the bottom" ([MyNASAData](https://mynasadata.larc.nasa.gov/globe-connections/globe-connections-air-quality)). The free GLOBE Observer app accepts cloud reports without a GLOBE ID ([GLOBE](https://www.globe.gov/web/s-cool/home/observation-and-reporting/report)), so Royal Crest could take part with just a phone. At South River High School in New Jersey, a climate club reports hourly station readings to the NWS ([Columbia Weather](https://columbiaweather.com/applications/education-climate-club/)).

UK schools follow RMetS/MetLink guidance, and a basic instrument set with a self-assembly screen costs about **£100 + VAT** ([RMetS](https://www.rmets.org/sites/default/files/2019-02/schoolwxstn.pdf)). They have shared readings on the Met Office's WOW site, as Didcot Girls' School does. Students there also use the school station as a "control" for microclimate fieldwork in the grounds ([MetLink](https://www.metlink.org/fieldwork-resource/a-weather-station-for-your-school/)). That design maps directly onto a ZIMSEC school-based project. The film should describe WOW in the past tense: the Met Office is **retiring the WOW website in 2026** ([Met Office](https://weather.metoffice.gov.uk/learn-about/met-office-for-schools/other-content/other-resources/are-you-obsessed-with-the-weather)), reportedly with "no direct replacement" ([weewx docs](https://weewx.com/docs/5.4/usersguide/uploading/)). The lesson for Royal Crest is to keep its own logbook, because online platforms can close.

The African examples matter more for this audience. **TAHMO** aims for 20,000 stations across sub-Saharan Africa, and "the majority of weather stations are being installed at local schools", where students also take manual readings for lessons and the data serves smallholder farmers ([METER Group](https://metergroup.com/case-studies/10-years-of-weather-network-building-in-africa/)). In Zimbabwe, Terre des Hommes Italy has set up **manual weather stations in 20 of 40 schools** with disaster-risk-reduction clubs in Chimanimani and Chipinge, including Birriiri High School, with support from the national meteorological service ([PreventionWeb](https://www.preventionweb.net/news/meet-high-school-activists-fighting-climate-change-zimbabwe)). There is also a real gap for school data to fill: the MSD reportedly had only about a quarter of roughly 1,400 stations working, and rural school and clinic stations once supplied local data ([PreventionWeb](https://www.preventionweb.net/news/official-data-dries-zimbabwes-farmers-gauge-rainfall-themselves)). That report probably dates from around 2015. No documented case was found of Zimbabwean school data guiding farmers' planting decisions, so the film should present that as a goal, not an achievement.

## Education 5.0 counts climate as heritage, which makes the station a five-mission project

Prof. Amon Murwira announced Education 5.0 in 2017 ([Frontiers in Education](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/education/articles/10.3389/feduc.2025.1634459/pdf)). It adds **innovation and industrialisation** to the "teaching, research, community service" missions of Education 3.0, which Murwira called "a colonial design" ([The Standard](https://thestandard.co.zw/local-news/article/200026068/minister-defends-heritage-based-curriculum)). Its foundation is heritage, and in Parliament Murwira listed the country's heritage endowments as minerals, water, plants, animals **and climate** ([Hansard](https://parlzim.gov.zw/?p=19911); snippet only, so the exact wording needs checking). For schools, Cabinet approved the Heritage-Based Education 2024–2030 framework, which was rolled out from 2024 ([The Herald](https://www.heraldonline.co.zw/cabinet-okays-heritage-based-framework/)) and is reportedly compulsory for all schools from 2027 ([Zimbabwe Situation](https://www.zimbabwesituation.com/news/heritage-based-curriculum-now-compulsory/)). Continuous assessment has been replaced by school-based projects ([The Independent](https://theindependent.co.zw/local-news/article/200026921/chaos-in-schools-over-new-curriculum)).

The station maps onto all five missions. Recording the local climate is **heritage**, and learners can set traditional weather signs alongside instrument readings, although no source shows ZIMSEC requires indigenous knowledge. Daily readings support **teaching** in Geography, Science and Mathematics. A multi-year dataset supports **research**, such as a microclimate study or an analysis of when the rainy season starts. Sharing rainfall totals with farmers and the School Development Committee is **community service**, following the Chipinge DRR model. Home-made gauges or a micro:bit data logger are **innovation** that fits the school innovation hubs and the coding training Minister Torerai Moyo has highlighted ([Bulawayo24](https://bulawayo24.com/index-id-news-sc-national-byo-264618.html)). **Industrialisation**, such as locally made gauges for farmer groups, should be presented as an aspiration. Sources give different lists of "pillars" for the schools curriculum ([The Herald](https://www.heraldonline.co.zw/heritage-based-education-a-pathway-to-economic-growth/)), so the film should use the five-mission version and attribute it to Education 5.0. It should also acknowledge that teachers have criticised how the rollout was handled ([NewsDay](https://newsday.co.zw/local-news/article/200027292/feature-zims-new-curriculum-throws-teachers-into-confusion)).

The prospectus mind-map can grow from a single trunk, "I read the weather station", into eight branches. Great Zimbabwe University in Masvingo is the nearest campus to Bikita, but its new GIS and climate degrees are known only from an aggregator site, so they need checking on gzu.ac.zw before the prospectus is printed. Public-sector vacancies are posted on the Public Service Commission portal ([PSC](https://erecruitment.psc.gov.zw/)).

## Conclusion

Whether the film works depends less on how many instruments it shows than on getting two Southern Hemisphere details right and on timing. A south-facing Stevenson screen door and a cumulonimbus storm instead of a British-style nimbostratus drizzle are what make the film accurate for Bikita rather than a copy of UK teaching material. The Cambridge 2027 changes mean the most durable sections are siting principles, climate-graph skills and the Zimbabwe rainfall story, all of which still serve climate-change content after instruments leave the Cambridge syllabus. ZIMSEC's working-station requirement, Education 5.0's naming of climate as heritage, and Zimbabwe's thin official observation network all point the same way. Royal Crest's station can be presented as a contribution to Masvingo's weather record, not only as an exam aid.

The careers message is more specific than "study geography". Keep Geography, but pair it with Maths and Physics if the goal is the one meteorology degree in the country, because the most direct route into the MSD runs through a physics department. Before production, six checks would remove most of the remaining uncertainty: the 2217 2027–2029 "Changes" page, the HBC 4022 syllabus instrument list, the RMetS siting figures (including where the barometer goes), the Cloud Atlas height bands, Bikita's Natural Region and rainfall from MSD records, and GZU's official programme titles.
