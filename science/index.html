The scientific method

How it works, step by step.

Every investigation, from a Form 1 practical to a university research project, follows the same six steps.

  1. 1

    Observe

    Notice something interesting in the world around you.

  2. 2

    Question

    Ask a clear question that can be tested.

  3. 3

    Hypothesise

    Predict what will happen, and say why.

  4. 4

    Experiment

    Test it fairly: change one variable and keep the others the same.

  5. 5

    Analyse

    Record results in a table, draw a graph and look for patterns.

  6. 6

    Conclude

    Answer the question, explain the result and share it.

Variables in a fair test

The ideas to master.

Use these words in your answers, projects and presentations.

Independent variable
The one thing you change on purpose.
Dependent variable
The thing you measure to see the effect.
Control variables
Everything you keep the same, so the test is fair.
Reliable results
Results that are similar when the experiment is repeated.
Anomaly
A result that does not fit the pattern of the others.
Hypothesis
A testable prediction with a scientific reason.

Lab safety: the golden rules

Stay safe, work well.

  1. Never enter the lab without a teacher.
  2. Wear goggles and a lab coat for practical work.
  3. Tie back long hair and tuck in ties.
  4. Never eat, drink or taste anything in the lab.
  5. Report every breakage, spill or injury at once.
  6. Read labels and hazard symbols before using a chemical.
  7. Point test tubes away from people when heating.
  8. Wash your hands when you finish.

Check yourself

Quick questions, tap for answers.

Q1In an investigation into how temperature affects how fast sugar dissolves, what is the independent variable?+
Answer

Temperature: it is the variable you change on purpose.

Q2Why do scientists repeat experiments?+
Answer

To check that the results are reliable and to spot any anomalies.

Q3What must you do first if you spill a chemical?+
Answer

Tell the teacher straight away.
