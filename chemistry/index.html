Ionic Bonding

Ionic bond (electrovalent)

⚡ Ionic bond (electrovalent): when atoms GIVE and TAKE electrons!

Sodium (Na 2,8,1) hands its 1 outer electron to chlorine (Cl 2,8,7). Now Na⁺ and Cl⁻ have opposite charges, held together by strong electrostatic attraction.

🔑 Electron transfer · Ions · Electrostatic attraction
🧂 Example: Na + Cl → Na⁺Cl⁻, sodium chloride (table salt)

Covalent Bonding

Covalent bond

🤝 Covalent bond: when atoms SHARE!

Two hydrogen atoms each bring 1 electron and share the pair between them. That shared pair is a covalent bond, and together they make H₂. ⚪⚪

🔑 Shared pair · Molecule · Non-metals
💨 Example: H + H → H–H (hydrogen gas)

Ions

Cation & anion

➕➖ Cation or anion? Here's how to never mix them up!

🔹 Cation (+): LOST electrons, usually a metal (Na⁺, Mg²⁺, Ca²⁺)
🔸 Anion (−): GAINED electrons, usually a non-metal (Cl⁻, O²⁻, S²⁻)
📏 Cations get smaller, anions get bigger

🐱 Memory tip: cats have paws, so CATions are PAWS-itive!

Electron Shells

The octet rule

🎱 The octet rule: atoms want a FULL outer shell!

Neon (2,8) is already full and very chill. 😎 Sodium (2,8,1) has 1 electron to give away; chlorine (2,8,7) needs just 1 more. That's why they react!

🔑 Valence electrons · Noble gas configuration · Duet rule (H and He need only 2)

Bond Types

Single, double, triple bonds

🔗 Single, double, triple: how many pairs are shared?

⚪ H–H: single bond, 1 shared pair
🔴 O=O: double bond, 2 shared pairs
🔵 N≡N: triple bond, 3 shared pairs

More shared pairs = a shorter, stronger bond. 💪
Bonus term: a lone pair is a pair of outer electrons NOT used in bonding.

Ionic vs covalent (quiz)

🆚 Ionic vs covalent: can you spot the difference?

🧂 Ionic (NaCl): electrons TRANSFERRED · metal + non-metal · high melting point · conducts when molten or dissolved
💧 Covalent (H₂O): electrons SHARED · non-metals · usually low melting point · most don't conduct
